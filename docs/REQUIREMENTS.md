# FarmerBook requirements register

This is the living source of truth for requirements supplied by the product owner. Codex updates it whenever a requirement is added, clarified, approved, implemented, verified, deployed, deferred, or rejected. `PLAN.md` remains the self-contained execution plan; `implementation-log.md` records completed evidence and deployment history.

## How requirement intake works

Codex asks one focused question at a time. A new answer is recorded first as `Proposed`. When the intended user, behavior, boundaries, and acceptance evidence are clear, it becomes `Approved`. Implementation moves it through `Implementing`, `Locally verified`, and `Live`. `Blocked` means a named external dependency prevents progress. `Deferred` and `Rejected` require an explicit reason and do not silently disappear.

Every implementation claim must cite observable evidence such as a test command, a browser journey, a database authorization check, or a deployed version. A feature that exists only in source is not `Live`. A feature hidden behind a disabled application or database control is not `Live`.

## Product objective

FarmerBook is the farmer-first social and professional network with a direct agriculture marketplace - LinkedIn-style identity and Facebook-style community, purpose-built for farmers. It should help Indian farmers build a trusted professional identity, share and learn with their community, reach customers and wholesalers, discover useful agricultural companies and offers, and safely receive practical help. The growth objective is to onboard many farmers and customers while preserving consent, privacy, marketplace trust, and production reliability. FarmerBook is designed for a future open-source release, but must not be described as open source until an approved licence and public source repository are in place.

## Identity and trust verification model

FarmerBook must not collapse every trust signal into one ambiguous blue tick. “A real person controls this account,” “this person is genuinely a farmer,” “this social account is established,” and “this seller completed trustworthy transactions” are different claims. The interface should show separate labels, each with its evidence type, last verification date, expiry where applicable, and a plain-language explanation.

The supported evidence paths should be:

- `Contact verified`: verified email and/or phone ownership. This proves control of a contact channel, not legal identity.
- `Passkey or trusted device secured`: strong account security and continuity. This does not prove who the person is.
- `Social account linked`: ownership proven through the platform's official OAuth flow, not by accepting a pasted profile URL.
- `Established social presence`: an agent evaluates account age, posting history, follower/engagement quality, identity consistency, and suspicious-growth signals. Follower count alone never becomes `Identity verified` because followers and engagement can be purchased.
- `Government identity verified`: an approved KYC provider validates an accepted identity document. FarmerBook should retain a provider receipt, result, document type, country, timestamp, and expiry rather than raw document images whenever the provider permits.
- `Liveness and face match verified`: an approved provider compares a consented live selfie/video challenge with the identity document. Raw biometric media requires strict purpose, retention, access, deletion, and incident controls.
- `Bank or payment-name verified`: an approved bank-account, UPI, or penny-drop provider confirms account ownership/name. This corroborates identity but does not prove the person is a farmer.
- `Organization verified`: an agricultural company, FPO, cooperative, NGO, government body, or institution is verified through official-domain email, registry/licence lookup, and an authorized representative.
- `Farmer or professional role verified`: evidence such as FPO/cooperative membership, farm lease/landholding or cultivation record, relevant licence/certificate, institutional record, or an approved field/remote assessment supports the claimed agricultural role. This is separate from identity proof.
- `Community vouched`: multiple already verified FarmerBook members or one approved FPO/cooperative representative attest to the profile, with conflict-of-interest, abuse, and revocation controls. This is a community signal, not government identity.
- `Live interview verified`: an authorized verifier completes a scheduled video or in-person check with a randomized challenge and records only the outcome and required audit evidence.
- `Address or service-area verified`: an approved document, postal challenge, field visit, or service-area registry supports the stated location without publishing a precise home or farm address.
- `Transaction reputation`: completed, traceable FarmerBook enquiries and eligible reviews show marketplace history. Reputation is not identity proof and must be labeled separately.
- `Risk reviewed`: duplicate-account, device, document-tampering, face-reuse, sanctions where legally required, and inconsistent-evidence checks determine whether to request another method, reject evidence, or suspend verification. A risk model never creates a positive identity badge on its own.

The initial visible states are `Not verified`, `Verification in progress`, `Contact verified`, `Social presence verified`, `Identity verified`, `Farmer role verified`, `Organization verified`, and `Verification expired or revoked`. A profile may hold more than one positive label. The AI agent may extract fields, compare consistency, detect likely tampering, explain missing evidence, and route the workflow; badge issuance must depend on explicit deterministic rules and signed provider/registry responses. Low-confidence or conflicting evidence fails closed as `Not verified` or requests a different verification path rather than inventing certainty.
